# Win on trust and retention, not services

The leading boosting sites do not beat a well-built marketplace on the boost catalogue. Rank boosts, placements and net wins are universal, and this app already covers LoL rank, wins, placements, normals, mastery and Arena plus Valorant and TFT. They win on **trust, conversion and retention**: public reviews they reply to quickly, 24/7 live chat, email or push updates, a configurator ETA, 10–15 payment methods, coupon codes, tiered wallet cashback (3–8%), referral links, and booster ratings with a "rehire" path. Most of these are small or medium builds on the existing Prisma ledger and Stripe Checkout, and they should come first. Two things change the priority order, though. First, **Riot's 2025–2026 Vanguard Anti-Boost program penalizes both account-shared and paid duo boosting**, and Riot reports 296,416 accounts actioned so far. That makes "safe", "VPN" and "ban-proof" marketing a liability rather than a selling point ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/vanguard-anti-boost)). Second, the app's strongest differentiator, Riot-API live tracking, runs on an unapproved development key, and Riot's developer policies make a production key for a boosting product very unlikely ([Riot Developer](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/2xko)). The roadmap therefore pairs quick trust and retention wins with risk work that is not optional: a tracking fallback that does not depend on the Riot API, an EU-compliant withdrawal and refund flow to replace "customers cannot cancel", region blocking for Korea, and dispute evidence for Stripe. It also lists features to avoid: account sales, fake scarcity and ban-safety claims.

A caveat on evidence: several competitor pages could not be loaded (the GameBoost homepage, Boosting Factory, Overgear, LoLBoost and parts of BoostRoyal and Eloking returned 403/404 errors or rendered only in JavaScript). Most feature claims also come from vendors' own marketing, not from independent verification. Effort sizes assume one full-stack developer: **S** is under a week, **M** is 1–3 weeks, **L** is more than 3 weeks.

The order workspace is already competitive. Eloking markets a "Members Area" that "lights up with live progress" and a booster who messages within 30 minutes. BoostRoyal advertises "Follow every match in real time from your dashboard" ([Eloking](https://www.eloking.com/); [BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/)). This app has realtime chat, audited encrypted credential sharing, pause/resume and per-minute rank, LP and match refresh, which matches or exceeds what those pages describe. None of the loaded competitor pages showed an LP-over-time graph or an order timeline. The gaps are almost entirely around the workspace:

## Conversion: price transparency, ETA and payment breadth close the sale

### The configurator should price LP gain and show a live ETA

The market-standard configurator asks for current tier and division, then desired tier and division, then current LP in 20-point buckets, an **LP-gain bracket that changes the price**, server, queue (Solo/Duo or Flex), and solo or duo mode. Add-ons are shown as "+X%" toggles. GameBoost uses LP-gain brackets of Very Low (1–15), Low (16–19) and Normal (20+) across 17 servers ([GameBoost](https://gameboost.com/league-of-legends/boosting/rank-boost)). Boosteria uses 10–19/20–24/25–29/30+ ([Boosteria](https://boosteria.org/elo-boost/prices)). LP-gain pricing matters because a low-gain account takes far more games for the same rank distance. Without it, boosters avoid those orders in the claim marketplace or the operator absorbs the loss. If the app's pricing engine does not already take LP gain and queue as inputs, adding them is an **S–M** change to the pricing function and the order schema.

Showing an ETA is the most visible missing element. GameBoost shows "1 day" for Silver I to Gold IV, and Boosteria shows a per-order "2 days 15 hours" and claims Turbo cuts it by about 20% ([GameBoost](https://gameboost.com/league-of-legends/boosting/rank-boost); [Boosteria](https://boosteria.org/elo-boost/prices)). Eloking instead publishes a "<30 minute starting time" badge and FAQ ranges of 3–7 days for Diamond to Master at about 8–10 games per day ([Eloking Wins](https://eloking.com/lol-boost/wins)). No site discloses its formula. A defensible model is games per division (scaled by the LP-gain bracket) divided by games per day, reduced by a priority multiplier. Calibrate it against this app's own completed-order history, which the finance ledger and order timestamps already hold. Show the same estimate in the order workspace as "projected completion" next to the Riot-API progress. **Effort S** for a static formula, **M** if it learns from history. Do not turn the ETA into a guaranteed deadline: GameBoost's partner terms say late delivery "may entitle the buyer to a full refund" ([GameBoost Partner FAQ](https://docs.gameboost.com/documentation/for-sellers/partner-faq)), and the app is not yet ready to absorb that.

### Paid add-ons follow a stable percentage ladder

Add-ons are percentage surcharges at nearly every site, and the price points cluster tightly:

| Add-on | Competitor pricing | Notes for this app |
|---|---|---|
| Priority / express | +25% GameBoost, +20% Boosteria Turbo | Use it to sort the booster claim queue and raise the booster's share |
| Stream games | +15–20% GameBoost, +15% Boosteria private link | A link field in the order workspace (S) |
| Solo-only queue | +20% LoL, **+60% Valorant** (GameBoost) | Valorant premium reflects five-stack norms |
| Coaching during boost | +15% LoL, +25% Valorant, +50% TFT (GameBoost) | Booster leaves notes or a VOD; ties to the coaching hedge below |
| Select a specific booster | +10% (Boosteria) | Needs booster profiles first |
| Champions/roles, agents | Free everywhere | Already offered |
| Offline mode / VPN | Free, sold as trust (Eloking, BoostRoyal) | See the risk section: present offline as a privacy option, never as ban protection |

Sources: [GameBoost LoL](https://gameboost.com/league-of-legends/boosting/rank-boost), [GameBoost Valorant](https://gameboost.com/valorant/boosting/rank-boost), [GameBoost TFT](https://gameboost.com/teamfight-tactics/boosting/rank-boost), [Boosteria](https://boosteria.org/elo-boost/prices) (Boosteria's mapping of percentages to add-ons was inferred by the fetcher and is unverified), [Eloking Wins](https://eloking.com/lol-boost/wins). Two pricing devices deserve copying. One is **net wins**, where "each loss is offset with 2 extra wins" so the customer always receives the wins they bought ([Eloking Wins](https://eloking.com/lol-boost/wins)). The other is a free automatic "+1 Bonus Win" on rank boosts ([GameBoost](https://gameboost.com/league-of-legends/boosting/rank-boost)). Both are cheap to express in the order model (a `targetNetWins` and a completion rule) and are **S** effort. On the catalogue, the only real gaps against competitors are a **Master+ LP product** that uses LP checkpoints instead of divisions ([Boosteria](https://boosteria.org/elo-boost/prices)), TFT Double Up (GameBoost, GGBoost, BoostRoyal), a Flex queue option (only Eloking), and **hourly coaching** (Eloking: 1–10 hours, individual or team) ([Eloking LoL](https://www.eloking.com/lol-boost)). Coaching matters more for risk reasons than for revenue, as explained below.

### Card-only, single-currency checkout is far below the market

Skycoach accepts PayPal, cards, Apple and Google Pay, EPS, iDEAL, Skrill, Neteller, Przelewy24, Rapid Transfer, several cryptocurrencies, Klarna and Sezzle, and offers a currency selector and four languages ([Skycoach](https://skycoach.gg/)). BoostRoyal lists 14 methods including paysafecard and Amazon Pay ([BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/)). Eloking offers nine languages ([Eloking](https://eloking.com/)). The audience skews young and often has no card, so wallets and local methods directly lift conversion. Stripe Checkout can switch on Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal (in supported regions), iDEAL, Bancontact, EPS, P24 and Klarna through payment-method settings, and it can present local currencies. That makes this an **S–M** change, mostly configuration plus webhook handling for delayed-notification methods like bank redirects. Confirm each method's availability for this merchant category with Stripe before relying on it. Crypto and paysafecard would need a second processor (**M–L**) and bring AML/KYC questions. Defer them. Eloking's pattern of **creating the account after payment** ("Password creation prompt after checkout") removes a signup wall ([Eloking](https://eloking.com/)). Because orders exist only once Stripe confirms payment, the webhook can create a NextAuth user from the Checkout email and send a set-password link. That is **M** effort and depends on email being in place.

## Trust: reviews, live support and notifications are the table stakes this app lacks

### Reviews are the single most-used trust device

Every leader shows a Trustpilot score with a large review count, and the active ones collect reviews continuously. GameBoost has **24,178 reviews, 8,884 of them in the last 12 months**, and invites customers to review. Skycoach has 28,801, with 8,024 in the last year, and replies to 99% of negative reviews, typically within 24 hours ([Trustpilot GameBoost](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gameboost.com); [Trustpilot Skycoach](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/skycoach.gg)). Recency is the differentiator. Eloking (30 reviews a year) and BoostRoyal (112 a year, flagged by Trustpilot for not inviting recently) have visibly stale profiles ([Trustpilot Eloking](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/eloking.com); [Trustpilot BoostRoyal](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/boostroyal.com)). BoostRoyal also runs its own on-site system: "4.86" from "79.4K customer ratings", with "Verified" tags ([BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/)).

The app should build a **post-completion flow**: a rating for the booster (communication and speed, as BuyBoosting scores them), then an optional tip, then a Trustpilot invite for satisfied customers. Ratings should be restricted to customers whose order the booster worked on, as BuyBoosting does ([BuyBoosting](https://buyboosting.com/boosters/)). In Prisma that means a `Review` model keyed one-to-one to a completed `Order`. Show verified reviews and an aggregate on the homepage and the service pages. **Effort M.** Do not offer incentives in exchange for removing reviews. One Skycoach reviewer alleges exactly this ([Trustpilot Skycoach](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/skycoach.gg)), and it is the kind of practice that turns a trust signal into a liability. Tipping (another Stripe Checkout session credited to the booster's ledger) is **S–M**, but no loaded competitor page documented it, so it is a possible differentiator rather than a catch-up item.

### Support complaints, not ban complaints, are what operators can control

Bans dominate 1-star reviews everywhere. The second cluster is controllable: "Dead support live chat and cannot find booster for 24h" at Eloking, "Booster is afk and dont respond", an order sitting "ready for pickup" for 48 hours, and slow starts at Skycoach ([Trustpilot Eloking](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/eloking.com); [Trustpilot Skycoach](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/skycoach.gg)). Leaders promise 24/7 live chat, and BoostRoyal adds phone ([BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/)). The cheapest catch-up is a hosted live-chat widget (Crisp, Tawk, Intercom) that hands off to the existing ticket system (**S**). The stronger move is to make the app's operational promises measurable: booster first message within 30 minutes of claim (Eloking's public promise), automatic release of unclaimed or unanswered orders, and a visible "booster away" state. These are covered under booster operations below.

### Email and push notifications are missing infrastructure

The app sends in-app notifications only. That means a customer who closes the tab never learns their order was claimed, paused or completed, which is exactly the "no communication" complaint that Boosting Factory's negative reviews center on ([Trustpilot BoostingFactory](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/boostingfactory.com)). BoostRoyal sends completion notifications and ships iOS and Android dashboard apps, and Skycoach promotes an app to "chat with a PRO on your phone" ([BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/); [Skycoach](https://skycoach.gg/)). The recommended path is to route the existing notification events through a transactional email provider (Resend or Postmark, with React Email templates) for claimed, first message, paused, game won or lost (digest), completed, and refund events (**S–M**). Next, make the app an installable PWA with Web Push (**M**). Native apps come **later (L)**. Email is also a prerequisite for post-payment account creation, review invites, and EU durable-medium confirmations.

### Booster profiles and "rehire" formalize what customers already do

Eloking and Skycoach do not let customers pick boosters, yet customers ask for specific ones anyway: "Stathis has been an incredible booster, and I always prioritize him." Skycoach tells reviewers to request a preferred PRO via live chat ([Trustpilot Eloking](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/eloking.com); [smartcustomer Skycoach](https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/skycoach.gg?page=8)). The richest model is BuyBoosting's directory. Each booster card shows a star rating with review count, completed orders, languages, an online dot (active in the last 15 minutes), open order slots and a Coach tag. A "Book" button falls back to the general pool if the order is not started within **four hours** ([BuyBoosting](https://buyboosting.com/boosters/)). BoostRoyal shows public profiles with rating and orders done ("4.94", "1,578 orders done") ([BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/)).

For this app, the staged path is: first, a **"request the same booster again"** button on completed orders, which creates the new order reserved for that booster with a timed fallback to the marketplace (**S–M**). Second, **booster profiles** built from data the app already has: completed orders, rating, languages, roles and champions (**M**). Third, a **bookable directory** with presence and slots, plus the +10% "select booster" surcharge Boosteria charges (**L**). Show public booster aliases only, never Riot IDs. A public list of boosters' accounts would be a ready-made enforcement list for Riot.

## Retention and monetization: a coupon engine and wallet cashback are the minimum

Wallet cashback with spend-based tiers is the dominant retention mechanic. BoostRoyal pays "up to 8%, based on your last 12 months" (Bronze 3%, Gold 5%, Diamond 6%, Challenger 8%), and Eloking stacks 3/5/7% cashback with 5/10/15% tier discounts, a friend discount and a daily lootbox ([BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/); [Eloking](https://eloking.com/)). Skycoach runs "Share your link, get $20!" plus an affiliate program ([Skycoach](https://skycoach.gg/)). GameBoost shows a discount-code field, "spend more get % off", and 3% cashback in coins on the configurator itself ([GameBoost](https://gameboost.com/league-of-legends/boosting/rank-boost)). Affiliate reference rates are about **5–10% revenue share**, and Eloboss offers "7% (or more) lifetime" ([Eloboss](https://eloboss.net/affiliate-program); [CueLinks](https://www.cuelinks.com/campaigns/gameboost-affiliate-program)).

Build the **coupon engine first (S)**. Stripe Checkout supports promotion codes natively. Mirror them in Prisma (a `Coupon` model with usage limits, an expiry and a per-user cap) so the configurator can preview the discount and so admins can issue codes as **complaint or ban compensation**, which is how BoostRoyal uses them ([Trustpilot BoostRoyal](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/boostroyal.com)). It is also the foundation for affiliate and influencer codes, an acquisition channel that review sites already use ([AskBoosters](https://askboosters.com/gameboost/)). Next comes a **customer wallet (M)** as new account types in the existing finance ledger: cashback credited on completion, spent at checkout by lowering the Stripe line-item amount or adding a one-off discount. Stripe restricts "stored value or credits ... issued by anyone other than the seller" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)). Self-issued, non-withdrawable store credit is the safe form, so never make it cash-out-able. **Referral links (S–M)** reuse the wallet. A self-serve **affiliate dashboard (M)** can wait. Bulk discounts for 10+ wins (Eloking) and spend thresholds such as Boosteria's extra 3% over $100 and 5% over $200 ([Boosteria](https://boosteria.org/elo-boost/prices)) are pricing-function changes (**S**).

Skip Eloking's "free daily lootbox spin" and Boosteria's "Offers for Today Left: 5" counter and 51% promo banner ([Eloking](https://eloking.com/); [Boosteria](https://boosteria.org/)). Randomized rewards sit close to gambling mechanics, which Stripe prohibits. A fake scarcity counter is exactly what Stripe's catch-all against businesses that are "unfair, deceptive, or predatory towards consumers" targets ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)). It also creates consumer-protection exposure, though that is an inference with no case law in the research.

## Booster operations: SLAs, slots and payout holds keep the marketplace honest

GameBoost is the only competitor with public partner rules, and they add up to an operating system the app's booster side partly lacks ([GameBoost Partner FAQ](https://docs.gameboost.com/documentation/for-sellers/partner-faq)). Partners must be 18+ and fully verified with ID and proof of residency before they can withdraw. They must reply to issues within **12 hours** and resolve them within **24 hours**, or staff may refund. Inactivity unlists them after 7 days and suspends them after 14, though a declared "seller break" prevents unlisting. Orders auto-complete after 24 hours of buyer silence. Withdrawals have a **€100 minimum**, method fees (bank 4% + €4) and an extra **30-day hold for new partners**. Disputes resolved for the buyer "can affect metrics and ranking", and "higher-ranked partners pay a lower platform fee." GameBoost also sends seller alerts through **Discord webhooks** and offers an API. BuyBoosting adds an "away" state and per-booster **order slots** ([BuyBoosting](https://buyboosting.com/boosters/)).

The most valuable additions, in order, are these. First, a **claim-and-respond SLA engine**: a scheduled job (Vercel Cron or a Supabase pg_cron function) that releases a claimed order back to the marketplace if the booster has not sent a first message within N minutes or started within N hours, and that escalates silent orders to admins (**M**). Second, **concurrent-slot limits and an away mode** on the booster profile, enforced in the claim route (**S**). Third, **Discord webhooks** for new claimable orders and new customer messages, an outbound POST per event (**S**). Fourth, a **new-booster earnings hold and minimum withdrawal** as ledger rules, which provides anti-fraud cover against boosters who take orders and abandon them (**S**). Fifth, **booster tiers** driven by rating, completion rate and disputes, which set claim priority (for example, priority orders visible first to top tiers) and the commission split (**M**). Industry commentary puts the organizer's cut at about **15–20%** of booster earnings ([Eloking on Medium](https://medium.com/eloking-news/how-much-do-boosters-make-for-boosting-in-league-of-legends-93f51b02ab4c)), and marketplaces such as Eldorado take 10% on boosting ([Eldorado](https://intercom.help/eldoradogg/en/articles/8409025-fees)). Finally, a formal **dispute workflow** with evidence (chat log, credential-reveal audit, match history) that feeds booster metrics (**M**). This evidence trail is also the app's best defense in Stripe chargebacks.

## Riot enforcement, EU consumer law and Stripe reshape what to build

### Duo, offline mode and VPN no longer reduce ban risk, so stop implying they do

Riot's ToS prohibits sharing login credentials and "playing on another person's account or otherwise engaging in activity intended to 'boost' an account's status or rank" (Sec. 1.5, 7.1(15)) ([Riot ToS](https://www.riotgames.com/en/terms-of-service)). Since Patch 25.18 (September 2025), LoL penalizes **both the boosted account and the booster** with a 3-day ban, then 14 days, then a permanent ban. It also reverts all boosted LP and MMR, revokes the season's ranked rewards, and links penalties across a player's accounts from November 2025. Riot states that **"duo boosting ... counts as boosting"** and "We're not just flagging accounts that share the same IP addresses" ([LoL Enforcement FAQ](https://leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/account-penalties-and-enforcement-faq)). The Vanguard Anti-Boost post (September 2026) extends this to Valorant. It covers paid queueing and hitchhiking, reports 296,416 accounts actioned across both games, says enforcement is "not even yet at full power", and flags TPM 2.0 attestation and more MFA restrictions as next ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/vanguard-anti-boost)). No TFT-specific penalty ladder was found.

This has direct product consequences. Competitors still market "Zero bans since 2021" (Eloking), "VPN-shielded sessions" and "Strict Offline Mode" (BoostRoyal) ([Eloking](https://eloking.com/); [BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/)), yet bans are the top 1-star complaint at all of them. BoostRoyal's own replies concede "No method can guarantee 100% protection" ([Trustpilot BoostRoyal](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/boostroyal.com)). The app should offer "appear offline" as a **privacy preference** (it hides the booster from the customer's friends list, and that is all it does), drop any VPN claims, and display a plain-language risk disclosure at checkout that cites Riot's penalties. A quotable ban policy, stating what is refunded and what is not, is a differentiator in a market where competitors' policies are vague. Hide or label duo as carrying the same Riot risk. Do not sell it as the "safe" option. Valorant and duo orders should carry the same disclosure.

### The Riot API dependency is the biggest technical risk in the app

Riot's developer policies require "All products must be registered in, and audited by Riot Games". They forbid products that "create an unfair advantage" or "alternatives for official skill ranking systems", and the 2XKO policy names "a boosting service disguised as coaching/training" as unacceptable ([Riot Developer General Policies](https://developer.riotgames.com/policies/general); [Riot Developer 2XKO](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/2xko)). A production key for this app is therefore very unlikely, and continued use of a development key in a commercial product puts the operator's Riot developer account at risk of termination. Development keys must also be regenerated every 24 hours and carry low rate limits, so per-minute auto-refresh for every active order will not scale. The live tracking panel is the feature most likely to break without warning.

The mitigation is architectural (**M**). Put tracking behind a provider interface. Cache aggressively (refresh on workspace view and on a slower cadence, not every minute per order). Add a fallback that does not depend on the API: the booster logs each game result and LP after every game, with an optional screenshot upload to Supabase Storage, and the customer confirms. Do not substitute scraping of third-party stat sites, which simply moves the ToS problem elsewhere. Do not build new features (ETA calibration, review verification, booster stats) that require the Riot API to work.

### Genuine coaching is the defensible hedge

The research ranks product lines from highest to lowest risk: account sales, account-shared boosting, duo boosting, remote or "cloud play" boosting (inferred, unsourced), then **coaching**. Coaching, meaning VOD review, live spectating, or normal games together without the coach playing ranked on or with the account, is not prohibited by the ToS text reviewed. Riot notes that playing normals or TFT together is allowed ([LoL Enforcement FAQ](https://leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/account-penalties-and-enforcement-faq)). Eloking already sells hourly coaching and "Hire a Teammate" with Session, Hours (1–10), Game Mode and Role inputs ([Eloking LoL](https://www.eloking.com/lol-boost)), and customers value boosters who "gave me tips on how to improve my own gameplay" ([Trustpilot Eloking](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/eloking.com)). A coaching product (hourly sessions, VOD review with timestamped notes in the existing chat, booster Coach tag) reuses the workspace and booster marketplace. It is the line most likely to survive both Riot escalation and processor underwriting (**M**). It must actually be coaching. Riot explicitly calls out relabeled boosting.

### "Customers cannot cancel" conflicts with EU consumer law

Under the EU Consumer Rights Directive, a service contract carries a **14-day withdrawal right**. The right is lost only once the service is **fully performed**, and only if the consumer expressly requested early performance and acknowledged the loss. A withdrawal after partial performance owes a proportionate refund ([CRD summary](https://compliance-ai-h.pages.dev/regulations/eu/consumer-rights-directive/withdrawal-rights); [EU e-Justice](https://webgate.ec.europa.eu/e-justice/639/EN/consumer_rights_directive_201183)). The CJEU's *Sky Österreich* ruling (July 2026) blocks using the digital-content waiver for digital services ([Covington](https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/cjeu-decides-when-streaming-subscriptions-are-subject-to-the-right-of-withdrawal/)). Directive 2023/2673 adds a mandatory online "withdraw from the contract here" button with a confirmation step and a durable-medium confirmation ([Dreyfus](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/03/06/consumer-law-what-are-the-rules-governing-the-three-click-right-of-withdrawal-in-the-european-union/)); its June 2026 application date was not verified. "No refunds once the booster starts" is therefore likely unenforceable against EU customers. The fix (**M**) has four parts: an express-consent and acknowledgement checkbox at checkout that is stored on the order; an email confirmation (another reason email is a prerequisite); a customer-facing **withdraw/cancel request** that computes a pro-rata refund from progress (divisions or wins delivered), with admin approval running through the existing refund flow; and published progress-based refund terms, which is how BoostRoyal frames its "progress-based refunds through a transparent dispute flow" ([BoostRoyal](https://www.boostroyal.com/)).

### Korea is a criminal-law line, and chargebacks are a processor line

South Korea has criminalized paid boosting since 2019, with penalties of up to **2 years' imprisonment or KRW 20M** ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/game-boosting-is-now-a-crime-in-south-korea-and-those-who-get-caught-could-go-to-jail-263976)). Remove the KR server from the configurator, block Korean billing countries in Stripe Checkout, and exclude Korea-resident boosters at application (**S**). No comparable law was found elsewhere.

Stripe does not name boosting. It does restrict "Sale of in-game currency or game items, unless the business is the operator of the virtual world", and it prohibits unfair or deceptive businesses ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)). The practical threat is a rising dispute ratio after Riot bans. Vendor sources put Stripe's high-risk zone at about **0.65–1%** of transactions disputed ([chargeback.io](https://www.chargeback.io/fr/blog/stripe-closed-suspended-my-account)). Riot's own post jokes that players should charge back boosters ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/vanguard-anti-boost)), and Riot can permanently ban accounts with chargebacks on Riot purchases ([Riot Support](https://support.riotgames.com/riot/billing/chargebacks)). Countermeasures include an automated Stripe dispute-evidence pack built from the audit trail (**M**), a clear statement descriptor, proactive partial refunds or coupon compensation on documented bans, and **not launching an accounts marketplace**. Riot says purchased accounts "will almost certainly be banned", and account sales are the line that fits Stripe's restricted category most directly ([LoL Enforcement FAQ](https://leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/account-penalties-and-enforcement-faq)). Crypto avoids chargebacks but brings AML/KYC obligations and does not remove EU withdrawal duties. It belongs in "later, if Stripe becomes untenable", not in the core plan.

## Prioritized roadmap

| Horizon | Item | Competitor precedent | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|

## Conclusion

The competitive set has converged on one recipe: the same catalogue, percentage add-ons, Trustpilot plus cashback, and safety claims that Riot has now publicly contradicted. That leaves an opening. An operator that is honest about Riot risk, refunds proportionately as EU law already requires, and resolves the controllable complaints (silent boosters, slow pickup, unanswered support) with enforced SLAs and email can earn the review volume that makes the other trust signals believable. The order workspace this app already has is a better foundation for that than most competitors show publicly.

The deeper implication is strategic. Riot's enforcement curve is rising (TPM attestation and MFA are next), and the app's showcase tracking depends on API access Riot is unlikely to grant. Every quarter of growth in shared-account and duo boosting therefore increases exposure to bans, chargebacks and processor review together. Building coaching, booster profiles and an API-independent progress layer now gives the business a version of itself that still works if boosting becomes untenable, and the reviews, wallet and booster reputation all carry over to it.
